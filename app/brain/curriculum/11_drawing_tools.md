# Drawing tools and chart craft

## Teaching voice
This module teaches the craft of the chart itself: how a professional places every tool, why each anchor was chosen, what makes the drawing provisional or confirmed, and what kills it. Every lesson draws the tool live on the chart, names the anchors with their real prices and times, and states the invalidation. The theme throughout: a drawing is a question you ask the market, and the close is the answer. Nothing drawn is a prediction.

## Lessons
### precision_anchors: Anchors and precision
- key points: Every line starts from a swing that exists in the data, wick or body, and you say which. Snapping to the candle's open, high, low or close keeps a drawing honest when you zoom. After a zoom, a resize or a timeframe change, re-check the anchors before you speak about the line. Say the timeframe the drawing belongs to.
- common mistakes: Drawing through the "look" of the chart instead of the swings. Describing a fifteen-minute line as if it mattered on the daily.
- best example: crypto, BTCUSDT, 1h, a clean trend with three visible swing lows.
- pattern_scanner: swing_low
- on_screen_takeaway: A drawing anchored on real swings survives a zoom. One anchored on a feeling does not.

### rays_and_extended: Trendlines, rays and extended lines
- key points: A trendline joins two swings; a ray keeps going to the right so you can see where price meets it later; an extended line runs both ways to find where the same slope mattered before. Two touches make a line, the third touch is the test. A close through the line is the invalidation; a wick through it is a visit.
- common mistakes: Steep lines through one swing. Redrawing the line every time price breaks it.
- best example: forex, EURUSD, 4h, a trend with at least three touches of the same line.
- pattern_scanner: higher_highs
- on_screen_takeaway: Two touches draw the line. The third touch tests it. A close breaks it.

### parallel_channel: Parallel channels
- key points: The base line goes through the swings on one side of the move; the parallel is a copy through the first clear swing on the other side. Price lives between the walls, and each touch that holds adds confidence. The first close outside a wall is the end of the channel and the start of a measured move the height of the channel.
- common mistakes: Putting the parallel through the single highest wick. Trading the middle of the channel.
- best example: crypto, ETHUSDT, 4h, a rising channel with two touches on each wall.
- pattern_scanner: higher_highs
- on_screen_takeaway: A channel is two walls. The exit, not the bounce, is the news.

### regression_channel: Regression channels
- key points: The channel is computed, not drawn: the middle line is the best-fit path through the closes of the range you choose, the bands sit a set number of standard deviations away. A touch of a band means price is stretched from its own average, which in a trend can last. Changing the range changes the whole channel, so always say the range.
- common mistakes: Fading every band touch. Comparing channels built on different ranges.
- best example: stock, SPY, 1h, a steady drift with a few band touches.
- pattern_scanner: none
- on_screen_takeaway: A regression channel is a statistic of the range you picked. Say the range.

### pitchfork_basics: Andrews pitchfork
- key points: Three pivots: the handle at the swing that started the move, then the next swing high and swing low. The median line runs from the handle through the midpoint of the other two; the tines are parallel through them. Price tends to return to the median after each tine touch. The fork is valid while price respects the median at least once.
- common mistakes: Picking three random points. Keeping a fork that price has never respected.
- best example: crypto, BTCUSDT, 4h, a trend that returned to its median line.
- pattern_scanner: swing_low
- on_screen_takeaway: Handle, two pivots, one median line. If price ignores the median, drop the fork.

### fib_extension: Fibonacci extensions
- key points: Three anchors: the start and end of the impulse and the end of the pullback. Levels project the impulse size from the pullback end: one-to-one, one point two seven two, one point six one eight. They are reference points where profit taking clusters, meaningful only when they land on a level that already mattered.
- common mistakes: Reading an extension as a target. Anchoring on a pullback that is not finished.
- best example: forex, GBPUSD, 1h, an impulse, a clean pullback, and a second leg.
- pattern_scanner: swing_low
- on_screen_takeaway: An extension is a ruler for the next leg, not a promise about it.

### fib_fan_speed: Fibonacci fans
- key points: Speed lines from a swing through the retracement points of the leg. They show whether a pullback is losing or keeping the pace of the impulse. A gentle tool, best used to compare the slope of a correction with the slope of the move it corrects.
- common mistakes: Treating fan lines as support. Using them on a flat market.
- best example: crypto, SOLUSDT, 4h, a strong leg and a slow pullback.
- pattern_scanner: swing_high
- on_screen_takeaway: Fan lines compare pace. Slope is information too.

### position_tool: Long and short position tool
- key points: Entry at the zone, stop beyond the invalidation of the idea, target at a real level. The tool draws the risk box in red and the reward box in green and prints the ratio. Position size equals the account risk divided by the distance to the stop. Change any price and the ratio and size recompute. On stream it is a ruler and an example, never a signal.
- common mistakes: Moving the stop to improve the ratio. Choosing a round-number target.
- best example: forex, EURUSD, 1h, a pullback to a zone with a swing low below it.
- pattern_scanner: swing_low
- on_screen_takeaway: The red box decides the size. The green box only tells you if the idea is worth it.

### measure_tool: Measuring moves
- key points: The ruler between two points shows the price change, the percent, the number of bars and the time. Measure the last few legs before you call the current one big or small. Measuring also tells you how long rests usually last, which calms the urge to act during one.
- common mistakes: Describing moves with adjectives. Measuring from a wick to a body without saying so.
- best example: crypto, BTCUSDT, 15m, a day with three clear legs.
- pattern_scanner: none
- on_screen_takeaway: Measure before you judge. Numbers over adjectives.

### fixed_range_profile: Fixed range volume profile
- key points: Pick the range first; the profile is volume by price inside it. The point of control is where the most business was done; the value area holds about seventy percent of the volume between the value area high and low. Fat areas are agreement, thin areas were fast moves and often get revisited. Move the range and everything moves.
- common mistakes: Comparing profiles of different ranges. Treating the POC as an exact level.
- best example: crypto, BTCUSDT, 1h, a two-day range with an obvious high-volume node.
- pattern_scanner: range
- on_screen_takeaway: The profile is only as honest as the range you chose. Say the range.

### anchored_vwap_use: Anchored VWAP
- key points: The volume-weighted average price since one chosen bar. Anchor on a swing, a session open or a news candle. Above the line the crowd that joined at the anchor is in profit and tends to defend pullbacks to it; below it they are under water and sell rallies into it. Two anchors on the chart show which crowd is winning.
- common mistakes: Anchoring at random. Using VWAP on data with no volume without saying so.
- best example: stock, AAPL, 15m, the session after an earnings gap.
- pattern_scanner: vwap_cross
- on_screen_takeaway: The anchor is the whole point. Pick the bar that mattered.

### studies_in_panes: Studies and their settings
- key points: Overlays go on the price (moving averages, Bollinger bands, VWAP); oscillators get their own pane (RSI, MACD, ATR). Say the settings: an EMA twenty is not an EMA fifty. Values on the open candle change until it closes; a signal is confirmed on the closed bar. Indicators describe price; the chart comes first and the indicator is the second opinion.
- common mistakes: Stacking five indicators that all read the same closes. Acting on an oscillator value that has not closed.
- best example: crypto, ETHUSDT, 1h, a trend with a visible RSI divergence.
- pattern_scanner: rsi_divergence
- on_screen_takeaway: Chart first, indicator second. Closed bars only.

### pattern_states: Patterns: forming, confirmed, failed
- key points: A pattern outline is dashed while it is forming. A close through the neckline or the boundary confirms it and the line turns solid. A close back inside after confirmation is a failed pattern, which is itself a strong piece of information. Keep the failed drawing on the chart, greyed, as the honest record.
- common mistakes: Calling a shape a pattern before the deciding close. Deleting failed drawings and pretending they never happened.
- best example: crypto, BTCUSDT, 4h, a double top with a clear neckline.
- pattern_scanner: double_top
- on_screen_takeaway: Dashed means forming. Solid means confirmed. Grey means it was wrong, and we keep it.

### triangle_wedge: Triangles and wedges
- key points: Converging highs and lows: symmetrical when both converge, ascending when the highs are flat, descending when the lows are flat. The height of the triangle at its start gives the scale of the move after the break. The break is a close outside the lines; the first wick through is often the fake.
- common mistakes: Choosing the break direction in advance. Drawing the lines through wicks that nobody traded.
- best example: forex, USDJPY, 4h, a symmetrical triangle that resolved.
- pattern_scanner: triangle
- on_screen_takeaway: The triangle says when, not where. Measure the height, wait for the close.

### head_shoulders: Head and shoulders
- key points: Three peaks, the middle one highest, shoulders at about the same height, neckline through the two dips. The pattern exists only after the right shoulder; it is confirmed only on a close below the neckline; the retest of the neckline from below is the second chance. Invalidation is a close above the head.
- common mistakes: Calling it at the head. Ignoring the retest.
- best example: crypto, ETHUSDT, 4h, a completed head and shoulders with a neckline retest.
- pattern_scanner: head_shoulders
- on_screen_takeaway: Three peaks, one neckline, one close. Then the retest.

### elliott_count: Elliott wave counts
- key points: Five waves with the trend, three against. The rules that keep a count honest: wave two never retraces all of wave one, wave three is never the shortest, wave four never enters wave one's price area. Label the waves on the zig-zag and say the rule that would kill the count. Keep the count until a rule breaks, then relabel once.
- common mistakes: Relabelling daily to fit the chart. Using waves as targets.
- best example: crypto, BTCUSDT, 1d, a clean five-wave advance.
- pattern_scanner: higher_highs
- on_screen_takeaway: A count lives by its rules. Say the rule that would kill it.

### sessions_keylevels: Sessions and key levels
- key points: The Asian, London and New York sessions each leave a high and a low; the next session often sweeps one before it chooses. The previous day and week high and low and the day and week open are the landmarks every desk watches. Draw few levels and watch the reaction rather than the line.
- common mistakes: Twenty levels on one chart. Ignoring the clock.
- best example: forex, EURUSD, 15m, the London open sweeping the Asian range.
- pattern_scanner: breakout_up
- on_screen_takeaway: Sessions leave extremes. The next session hunts them.

### alerts_not_signals: Alerts and what they mean
- key points: An alert on the deciding level means you do not have to stare at the screen. When it fires the job begins: watch the candle close against the level. A touch is a touch; a close is a decision. An alert is a reminder, not a trade.
- common mistakes: Acting on the ring. Setting alerts on round numbers nobody traded.
- best example: crypto, BTCUSDT, 15m, an alert at the previous day high.
- pattern_scanner: resistance_level
- on_screen_takeaway: The alert rings. The close decides.

### chart_hygiene: Chart hygiene
- key points: Every drawing has a name and a job; update the drawing with the same name instead of adding a copy. Remove what no longer serves the story, but keep invalidated drawings greyed as the record. A readable chart has fewer than ten objects. If there is nothing to draw, draw nothing and say why.
- common mistakes: Twenty overlapping lines. Deleting the losers.
- best example: crypto, BTCUSDT, 1h, any recent window.
- pattern_scanner: none
- on_screen_takeaway: Few objects, each with a job. Keep the wrong ones, greyed, as the record.
