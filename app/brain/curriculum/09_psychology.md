# Trading psychology

## Teaching voice
Psychology lessons should still be anchored to the chart: show the candle where fear of missing out kicks in, the pullback where revenge trades happen, the range where boredom trades appear. Speak with empathy and without preaching. Make clear that no mindset produces profits; good habits only reduce self-inflicted damage.

## Lessons
### fomo_chasing: Fear of missing out and chasing
- key points: FOMO is strongest after a large green candle, exactly where risk is highest because the invalidation is far away. Chasing means entering with no logical stop nearby. A move you missed is information, not an obligation. Waiting for a pullback or the next setup costs nothing.
- common mistakes: Buying the top of an extended candle. Reasoning that "it will keep going".
- best example: crypto, SOLUSDT, 15m, an extended run of green candles followed by a sharp pullback.
- pattern_scanner: breakout_up
- on_screen_takeaway: The move you missed is gone. Chasing it means entering where the stop makes no sense.

### revenge_trading: Revenge trading after a loss
- key points: After a loss, the urge to recover quickly leads to oversized or unplanned trades. The second loss is usually bigger than the first. A rule such as a pause after a losing trade removes the decision from the emotional moment. Losses are a cost of the process, not a personal failure.
- common mistakes: Re-entering immediately at a worse level. Doubling size to "get back to even".
- best example: forex, GBPUSD, 15m, a stop-out followed by a fast move in the original direction, the classic re-entry trap.
- pattern_scanner: pin_bar_bear
- on_screen_takeaway: The trade after a loss is the most dangerous one. Pause before taking it.

### overtrading_boredom: Overtrading and boredom
- key points: Ranges and quiet sessions tempt traders to force setups that are not there. More trades does not mean more opportunity; it means more fees and more noise. A written checklist filters boredom trades. Doing nothing is a valid position.
- common mistakes: Trading the middle of a range. Lowering the standard for a setup because nothing has happened.
- best example: forex, USDJPY, 1h, a tight range with many small candles where no clean setup exists.
- pattern_scanner: range
- on_screen_takeaway: No setup is a setup. Boredom trades are paid for with fees and attention.

### loss_aversion: Loss aversion and moving stops
- key points: Losses hurt more than equal gains feel good, so traders widen stops to avoid realising a loss. The small planned loss becomes a large unplanned one. The invalidation was chosen with a clear head; the urge to move it comes from a stressed one. Honour the original level.
- common mistakes: Moving the stop further away "to give it room". Removing the stop entirely.
- best example: crypto, ETHUSDT, 4h, a trade whose invalidation is hit and price keeps falling well beyond it.
- pattern_scanner: breakout_down
- on_screen_takeaway: A stop moved further away is a decision made under stress. Respect the one made with a clear head.

### confirmation_bias: Confirmation bias and indicators
- key points: Once you want a trade, you notice evidence for it and ignore evidence against it. Adding indicators that agree is the most common form. Write down what would make the idea wrong before entering. Seek the counter-argument on purpose.
- common mistakes: Searching timeframes until one agrees. Dismissing a bearish structure because an indicator is oversold.
- best example: stock, TSLA, 1d, a chart where price structure is bearish while an oscillator reads oversold.
- pattern_scanner: rsi_oversold
- on_screen_takeaway: Decide what would prove you wrong before you enter. Then look for it honestly.

### process_over_outcome: Process over outcome
- key points: A good trade can lose and a bad trade can win; the outcome of one trade says little. Judge decisions by whether the plan was followed. Keep a journal with the reason for entry, the invalidation and whether the rules were respected. Consistency is measured over many trades, not one.
- common mistakes: Changing a method after a few losses. Feeling confident after a lucky win.
- best example: crypto, BTCUSDT, 4h, two similar setups where one works and one is stopped out.
- pattern_scanner: engulfing_bull
- on_screen_takeaway: Judge the decision, not the outcome. One trade proves nothing either way.
