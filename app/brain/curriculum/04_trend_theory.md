# Trend, Dow theory and Wyckoff basics

## Teaching voice
Respect the classics without mythology. Dow and Wyckoff were describing how crowds move price a century ago, and the descriptions still hold because people have not changed. Teach the ideas as lenses for reading a chart, show them on real price, and skip the secret-society language. Nothing here forecasts; it helps you tell which phase the market is probably in.

## Lessons
### dow_three_trends: Dow theory: the three trends
- key points: Dow described a primary trend (months to years), secondary reactions against it (weeks), and minor daily noise. A pullback on the daily chart can be a full downtrend on the 15-minute. Always know which trend you are looking at. The primary trend is the hardest to fight.
- common mistakes: Trading minor noise as if it were the primary trend. Mixing timeframes without noticing.
- best example: stock, MSFT, 1d, a clear multi-month uptrend containing at least two visible secondary pullbacks.
- pattern_scanner: higher_highs
- on_screen_takeaway: Every chart holds three trends at once. Decide which one you are trading before you act.

### trend_phases: Accumulation, participation, distribution
- key points: Dow's three phases: accumulation (quiet, informed buying after a decline), public participation (the obvious trend), distribution (informed selling into enthusiasm). Volume often rises in participation and gets erratic in distribution. You usually recognise a phase only after it is well underway.
- common mistakes: Assuming you can spot accumulation in real time. Calling the top at every pause.
- best example: crypto, BTCUSDT, 1d, a long quiet range followed by a sustained uptrend and then a choppy top.
- pattern_scanner: range
- on_screen_takeaway: Markets move in phases. Spotting the phase is easier in hindsight, so stay humble.

### volume_confirms: Volume confirms the trend
- key points: Dow said volume should expand in the direction of the primary trend. Rallies on rising volume and pullbacks on shrinking volume are healthy. Rallies on thin volume with heavy sell-off volume warn that the trend is tired. Volume is context, not a trigger.
- common mistakes: Reading a single volume bar as a signal. Ignoring that crypto volume differs across exchanges.
- best example: stock, SPY, 1d, an uptrend where up days show higher volume bars than down days.
- pattern_scanner: volume_spike
- on_screen_takeaway: In a healthy trend, volume grows with the trend and fades on pullbacks.

### wyckoff_accumulation: Wyckoff accumulation basics
- key points: Wyckoff's accumulation schematic: a selling climax, an automatic rally, secondary tests, a possible spring below the range low, then a sign of strength breaking out. The spring is a trap for late sellers. The structure is a template, and real markets rarely match it cleanly. Invalidation is a decisive close below the range after the spring.
- common mistakes: Forcing every range into the schematic. Buying a "spring" that is just a breakdown.
- best example: crypto, ETHUSDT, 4h, a range after a decline where price wicks below the range low and closes back inside within 2 candles.
- pattern_scanner: support_level
- on_screen_takeaway: Wyckoff accumulation is a template for a base; the spring and the breakout are the two events to study.

### wyckoff_distribution: Wyckoff distribution basics
- key points: The mirror image: buying climax, automatic reaction, secondary tests, an upthrust above the range high (a trap for late buyers), then a sign of weakness breaking down. Upthrusts show demand failing where it should have succeeded. Invalidation is a sustained close above the upthrust high.
- common mistakes: Shorting every poke above a range. Labelling a continuation range as distribution.
- best example: stock, TSLA, 1d, a range after a rally where price pokes above the range high and closes back inside.
- pattern_scanner: resistance_level
- on_screen_takeaway: An upthrust is a failed breakout at the top of a range. Failure where strength was expected is the message.

### effort_vs_result: Effort versus result
- key points: Wyckoff's law: large effort (volume) should produce a proportional result (price movement). High volume with little price progress means the other side is absorbing. This shows up as wide-range volume with small candle bodies at the edge of a move. It is a warning sign, not a timing tool.
- common mistakes: Acting on one bar of divergence. Forgetting that news spikes distort volume.
- best example: crypto, BTCUSDT, 4h, a high-volume candle with a small body near a swing high after a rally.
- pattern_scanner: volume_spike
- on_screen_takeaway: When big volume produces small movement, someone is absorbing. Treat it as a warning, not a trigger.
