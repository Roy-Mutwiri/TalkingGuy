# Multiple timeframes, replay and live behaviour

## Teaching voice
This module is about honesty with time: reading the big chart for context and the small chart for timing, practising on hidden history without peeking, and understanding why a drawing that looks perfect on a finished chart was provisional while it printed. Theo keeps every observation tied to its timeframe and its moment, says what is confirmed and what is still forming, and shows the difference on the chart.

## Lessons
### top_down: Top-down analysis
- key points: Start on the higher timeframe for the direction and the levels that matter; drop to the lower timeframe for structure and timing. The higher timeframe wins ties. A lower-timeframe setup against a higher-timeframe level is a counter-trend trade and should be named as one.
- common mistakes: Taking a fifteen-minute signal into a daily wall. Mixing the levels of two timeframes without labels.
- best example: crypto, BTCUSDT, 4h, a daily level with a clean lower-timeframe reaction.
- pattern_scanner: resistance_level
- on_screen_takeaway: Big chart for direction, small chart for timing. The big chart wins ties.

### same_moment: Keeping analysis at the same moment
- key points: Every drawing belongs to the moment it was made. When the chart updates, say what changed: which drawing is still valid, which is now confirmed, which was invalidated. Separate the broad context that changes slowly from the entry conditions that change every candle.
- common mistakes: Describing a level drawn at the open as if it were drawn now. Moving the goalposts after the fact.
- best example: forex, EURUSD, 15m, a session where a level was tested twice.
- pattern_scanner: support_level
- on_screen_takeaway: Say when the drawing was made and what has changed since.

### bar_replay: Bar replay practice
- key points: Hide the right edge and reveal candles one at a time. Decide out loud at each bar, then reveal. Record the misreads as carefully as the good reads; the record of mistakes is the lesson. Finished charts make everything look obvious; replay restores the uncertainty that was real.
- common mistakes: Peeking. Counting only the wins.
- best example: crypto, BTCUSDT, 1h, a window with a failed breakout followed by a real one.
- pattern_scanner: breakout_up
- on_screen_takeaway: Decide before you reveal. Keep the misreads.

### forming_vs_confirmed: Forming versus confirmed
- key points: Studies and swing labels on the open candle change until it closes; the newest swing needs bars after it to exist at all. A higher-timeframe value is confirmed only when that higher-timeframe bar has closed. Drawings are dashed while forming and solid once the deciding close has printed.
- common mistakes: Acting on an RSI value of the open candle. Trusting the newest swing label.
- best example: crypto, ETHUSDT, 15m, a live session with the last candle still printing.
- pattern_scanner: none
- on_screen_takeaway: The open candle can change everything. Confirmed means closed.

### repainting_honesty: Repainting and hindsight
- key points: A signal that uses information from after the bar it is drawn on would not have been visible live; backtests that allow it show impossible results. Intrabar data differs between sources, so volume-by-price and footprint views can change after the fact. Say what was knowable at the time.
- common mistakes: Trusting a strategy report that peeked. Claiming a pattern was obvious live when it needed later bars.
- best example: stock, SPY, 15m, a day with a late-confirmed swing.
- pattern_scanner: none
- on_screen_takeaway: Only what was knowable at the time counts. Everything else is hindsight.

### testing_assumptions: Testing with honest assumptions
- key points: A test of an idea needs explicit rules, costs and slippage, and a period of data the rules were not built on. A result without costs is fiction. Report the losing streaks and the biggest drawdown, not the total return.
- common mistakes: Tuning the rules to the same data they are judged on. Forgetting fees.
- best example: crypto, BTCUSDT, 1h, a simple rule shown on a window the rule was not tuned on.
- pattern_scanner: sma_cross
- on_screen_takeaway: Costs, slippage and unseen data. Otherwise it is a story, not a test.
